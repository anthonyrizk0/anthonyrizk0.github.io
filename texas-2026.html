How to read this guide

Values and character are not the same thing. People can sincerely disagree on policy; allegations of corruption, dishonesty, or discriminatory conduct deserve separate scrutiny.

Party affiliation is not proof of a person’s integrity. For lesser-known candidates, some entries describe broad political orientation because an individual public record is thin.

Important: The countywide list includes different congressional and legislative districts. You will only vote in races corresponding to your own registered address. Every race in this guide is contested, but not necessarily electorally close.

This guide is a readable starting point, not an endorsement or an independently fact-checked voter guide. For consequential decisions, verify claims and check primary sources and current candidate platforms.

Section 07

Uncontested Positions & Making Your Decision

Uncontested positions

The following races are uncontested in the 2026 Travis County general election, meaning only one candidate appears on the countywide candidate list.

These include Sheryl Cole in State House District 46; numerous Travis County district judges; County Judge Andy Brown; the county court-at-law judges; the probate judge; district and county clerks; the county treasurer; county commissioners; justices of the peace; and the county constable position.

You can still vote for these candidates or leave those races blank. Voting in one race does not require voting in every race.

How to evaluate candidates’ morals and values

When evaluating political candidates, it’s useful to distinguish between three things: what they believe, how they’ve behaved, and whose interests they prioritize.

Rather than simply asking whether someone is a “good person,” consider a few more specific questions:

QuestionWhat to look for
Do they tell the truth?Documented dishonesty, misleading statements, transparency
Do they respect other people?Treatment of immigrants, minorities, LGBTQ+ people, and political opponents
Do they respect individual autonomy?Reproductive freedom, religious liberty, privacy, medical decisions
Do they protect vulnerable people?Policies affecting children, people with disabilities, and low-income residents
Do they abuse power?Corruption allegations, conflicts of interest, retaliation, misuse of public office
Do they govern competently?Results, leadership, willingness to compromise, accountability for failures

One important distinction: a candidate may hold values you strongly disagree with, but that doesn’t automatically make them personally dishonest or corrupt. Conversely, a candidate whose positions you support can still behave unethically.

Political values, personal integrity, and governing competence are related, but they aren’t interchangeable.

Useful resources

These nonpartisan and independent resources can help you investigate candidates’ positions and records beyond brief summaries.

  • VOTE411 — League of Women Voters — Candidate questionnaires, voting information, and explanations of ballot contests.
  • The Texas Tribune — Independent reporting on Texas candidates, voting records, policy positions, controversies, and campaign funding.
  • Ballotpedia — Candidate biographies, election histories, campaign information, and legislative records.
  • VoteTexas.gov — Official Texas voting information, including registration, polling places, and election procedures.

You can bring printed notes or a marked-up sample ballot when voting. Texas generally prohibits using phones and other wireless communication devices within 100 feet of a voting station, so preparing your choices beforehand is useful.

Making your own decision

A useful starting point is to identify three or four values that matter most to you, then examine how the candidates’ records and policy positions align with them.

Consider not only what candidates promise during campaigns, but what they have actually done when given responsibility or power.

No candidate will necessarily align perfectly with your beliefs. Voting often involves weighing competing priorities, evaluating credibility, and deciding which differences matter most.

The goal isn’t necessarily to find a candidate you agree with on everything. It’s to make an informed choice that reflects your own values, using evidence rather than party labels alone.
